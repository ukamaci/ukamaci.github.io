About

I defended my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in July 2026, advised by Prof. Farzad Kamalabadi in the Coordinated Science Laboratory. My thesis develops learned reconstruction and uncertainty quantification methods for spectral imaging, using diffusion models and posterior sampling.

I also built the CNN artifact-removal pipeline and the data-quality flags used in the operational science pipeline of NASA's ICON mission. Before Illinois, I received my BS in Electrical and Electronics Engineering from Middle East Technical University in 2017.

I am interested in generative models, computer vision, and inverse problems, and in taking these methods from research to real-world applications.

Research

  • Diffusion models for inverse problems

    Diffusion posterior sampling for a nonlinear spectral imaging problem, with one unsupervised prior reused across measurement configurations.

  • Uncertainty quantification

    Deep ensembles of conditional diffusion models that produce calibrated per-pixel error bars.

  • Learned reconstruction

    A deep learning solver for solar slitless spectroscopy that halves velocity error relative to the classical solver and runs 60x faster.

  • Space instruments

    Artifact removal and data-quality assessment for NASA ICON, and measurement design for diffractive and multi-frame satellite imaging.
